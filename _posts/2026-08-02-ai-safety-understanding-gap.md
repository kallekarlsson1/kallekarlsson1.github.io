AI and especially LLMs could turn out to be the most consequential technology that we humans have introduced in the last 100 years. With this comes a range of consequences, from sweeping high unemployment in high paying jobs to AI being used my malicious actors to assert harm against people or organizations in various ways. The latter part is what the field of AI safety is grappling with. The discoruse in the AI safety community seems to mostly be about: how do we make better models ? (in the sense that they do not advise or partake in illgeal or harmful activies) or how do we make sure that actors we classify as harmful actors do not get access to these models or the hardware to make them? This is good and we urgenlty need more research and more engagement from people inside or outside the community to address these challenges. But something I belived has been lost or have not gotten the attention it deserves is the implication of non-malicious actors taking decisions based or augmented by AI without fully understanding it shortfalls and biases.
